A proposal for joint research with K-State and Nebraska

The problems in this field are not solved by software alone. They need measured field data, instrumented rigs and the people who inspect grain facilities for a living. This page proposes specific research questions, names the university units that fit them, and states what is not yet verified.

01

Why these two universities

Both are land-grant institutions sitting inside the region where the problem is concentrated, and both already publish in this subject.

Kansas State University

K-State runs the units closest to this work: Grain and Food Science with its own pilot facilities and feed mill, the Advanced Manufacturing and Bulk Solids Technology Center in Olathe, and K-State Research and Extension's agricultural safety programme, which already publishes Standards of Practice, forms and checklists used in the field.

University of Nebraska–Lincoln

UNL brings Biological Systems Engineering — the joint agriculture-and-engineering department — mechanical engineering, Nebraska Innovation Campus, and Nebraska Extension, which already publishes grain bin safety and engulfment material.

The two institutions already cross-publish in this exact subject: Nebraska Extension has republished a K-State scientist's grain-dust-explosion question-and-answer piece. A two-university project is therefore not an artificial structure.

Why it matters commercially

  • Validation is the difference between a tool people try and a tool people rely on.
  • Field access is the one thing a two-person team cannot buy.
  • Reviewers who inspect facilities for a living can tell us whether an encoded rule matches what an inspector actually asks.
  • Students are a research pipeline and a hiring pipeline at the same time.

Units above were checked on their own live pages in October 2026. No individual is named, because none has been approached and no directory lookup establishes a project owner.

02

Three research questions

Each is stated so that it can fail. A question that cannot fail is not research.

QuestionWhat each side bringsWhere it would run
1 Do published transport-velocity criteria hold against measured field data?
In a working grain-handling dust system, does measured branch velocity match the velocity predicted from cited fitting-loss tables, and do the differences localise to particular fitting types?		 We bring: the 3D designer, the predicted per-branch loss, and exported geometry. The validation we hold is limited: within 3% on a redacted real two-pickup dust system (smooth-elbow method; per-component losses still differ. They bring: an instrumented rig or a cooperating facility, traverse instrumentation, and a graduate student under a documented protocol. K-State's bulk-solids technology centre in Olathe for testing; UNL Biological Systems Engineering for field access.
2 Can dust-capture performance at transfer points be predicted well enough to size it instead of guessing?
For a defined transfer point, does measured capture match the capture volume predicted from hood geometry and airflow, and how sensitive is it to cross-draft?		 We bring: the 3D designer and a headless open-source CFD pipeline driven from the same geometry. They bring: a test stand with controllable cross-draft, tracer instrumentation, and real facilities through extension networks. K-State Grain and Food Science pilot facilities and mechanical engineering for the CFD method; UNL Biological Systems Engineering.
3 Can jurisdiction rules be encoded as machine-checkable records that agree with what inspectors ask?
When OSHA 29 CFR 1910.272 and state adoptions are encoded, what fraction of rules are computable from facility data, procedural only, or not computable — and does an experienced inspector agree with the classification?		 We bring: the rule schema and the three-valued output. They bring: extension safety specialists as independent reviewers and a validation panel. K-State Research and Extension agricultural safety; Nebraska Extension.

Fourth topic, deliberately deferred. Explosion-vent siting and keep-clear distances are a real question, but answering it properly needs vented-deflagration test capability that we could not confirm exists at either institution. Until that is confirmed, it stays a desk-study and CFD topic, not a physical test programme.

03

The first project we would propose

Question three, not question one. It is the only one that can start in a semester without equipment, capital or a plant shutdown.

Scope of work

  1. Define the jurisdiction set, starting with Kansas and Nebraska, and document exactly which authority adopts which rule.
  2. Transcribe the primary regulatory text from the authoritative source.
  3. Encode the rules into the schema against a written encoding convention.
  4. Blind-classify every rule as computable, procedural checklist, or not computable with the missing inputs named.
  5. Have two independent reviewers — one from each state's extension service — classify the same rules without seeing our answers.
  6. Measure the agreement and publish the disagreement list.

Deliverables

  • A machine-readable rule set with per-rule citations and a versioned encoding convention.
  • An inter-rater agreement report, including the disagreements, which are the most useful output.
  • A short methods paper suitable for an extension publication or a professional-society conference.
  • A public, citable validation dataset: jurisdiction, rule, classification, reviewer vote.

Who owns what

  • KJLabs: the schema, the software, the encoding labour, the dataset structure, the product decision.
  • University: expert reviewers, institutional review of the protocol, the student, the academic framing.
  • Written down before work starts. Not assumed in conversation.

UNL publishes two industry research templates on its Industry Relations pages — an exclusive-rights path for a fee, and a standard path with an option period and no fee at execution. K-State's equivalent terms were not found published. Modelling a first project on the standard path is the conservative default for a two-person company.

04

Students, and how a project becomes a programme

Capstone and class projects

Both universities list industry class projects and student competitions as engagement routes. A well-scoped encoding or measurement task fits a semester and produces something the university can count.

Graduate research assistantship

A funded assistantship is what turns a one-semester courtesy project into a multi-year programme, and it is the natural budget line in a federal or state research proposal.

Extension specialists

The reviewers in question three are practitioners. Their disagreement with our classification is the deliverable, not a problem to be managed.

How it could be funded

Only programmes whose own pages we could read are listed, with the eligibility catch stated.

NSF SBIR / STTR Phase I awards up to $305,000 and Phase II up to $1,250,000, per the NSF programme page; NSF states it takes no equity. The company applies directly. This is the most direct route for a small team.

NSF I-Corps Teams Up to $50,000 over twelve months per the solicitation, with cost sharing built into the budget structure. The university is the applicant, not the company. The regional route through Nebraska I-Corps is the accessible on-ramp.
Nebraska Academic R&D Grant Phase I capped at $100,000 and Phase II at $400,000 with a one-to-one match, per the state programme page. Requires physical operations in Nebraska, which a Kansas company does not have today. An eligibility catch, not a rounding error.
Nebraska SBIR/STTR match Phase 0 up to $5,000 and later phases up to $150,000 or $300,000, per the state programme page. Requires the majority of the work to be performed in Nebraska.

Programme facts move. Every figure above is quoted from the programme's own page as read in October 2026 and must be re-checked before anything is submitted. Where a page could not be read, this table says so instead of filling the gap.

06

What we have not verified

Stated here so that no university or advisor has to discover it for us.

  • Whether the Olathe bulk-solids centre's filtration and materials-handling equipment is commissioned. Its own page described systems as completing in 2025.
  • K-State's sponsored-research terms — intellectual property, indirect cost rate and minimum project size were not found published.
  • No centre dedicated to combustible dust or process safety was found at either university.
  • No named faculty owner for any of these questions. Naming one without a directory lookup would be a guess.
  • Whether either institution can host vented-deflagration testing.
  • The current Kansas SBIR/STTR matching programme terms — the state page returned empty content when read.

Our three asks of each

Kansas State University

  • Is the Olathe filtration and conveying equipment commissioned, and what is the minimum engagement for a two-person pre-revenue company?
  • Will an extension safety specialist blind-classify our rule set so the disagreement list can be published?
  • What are K-State's sponsored-research and intellectual-property terms?

University of Nebraska–Lincoln

  • For a validation study with no expected patentable output, which published agreement path is appropriate, and what is the smallest agreement you would execute?
  • Is Nebraska I-Corps a route to a research relationship, or customer-discovery training only?
  • Is there a graduate student with instrumentation who could run documented duct traverses on a cooperating facility?

A proposal for discussion. Nothing here is a commitment, a funded project, an agreement, or a claim that either university has been contacted. No individual is named because none has been approached.
