Briefing · 05

How a plan would be framed. Not a plan.

This page exists to show the shape of the thinking and the inputs a real plan would need. It uses algebraic placeholders instead of invented figures, because a made-up number on a public page is worse than an obvious blank.

Read this first Aspirational — not an actual plan

Nothing on this page is a forecast, a target, a projection or a commitment. No revenue, price, cost, headcount or market-size figure is stated, because none has been established. Where a real plan would carry a number, this page carries a placeholder and names the evidence that would replace it.

What this page isWhat it is not
A worked example of plan structure, so an advisor can react to the shape of the business before we have numbers.A business plan, an operating plan, or a document anyone should file, fund or act on.
A list of the specific evidence we would need to collect, and who could help collect it.Evidence itself. Every figure that matters is still missing, and this page says so.
A way to expose the assumptions early enough that being wrong is cheap.A valuation, an offer, a solicitation, or a claim on any market.
01

The frame

Nine blocks. Each one states what belongs there, and what we would have to find out to fill it honestly.

BlockWhat belongs in itWhat we still have to establish
ProblemThe specific, sourced failure that costs someone money, time or safety today.Which of the problems we can describe is the one a buyer will actually pay to fix, and what it costs them now.
CustomerThe narrowest first customer: role, facility type, size, and who signs.Customer discovery. We have a domain expert and a pilot, not interviews with buyers.
OfferWhat is delivered, in what unit, with what the customer gets that they cannot get from a spreadsheet.Whether the first offer is a service, a seat, a site licence, or an evidence package.
Pricing hypothesisHow value is anchored — per seat, per facility, per project, or per review cycle.Willingness to pay, tested against a real alternative price: the consultant, the spreadsheet, or nothing.
ChannelHow the first ten customers hear about it.Whether that is equipment manufacturers, engineering firms, co-ops and elevators directly, or university and extension networks.
Cost structureThe recurring cost of serving one more customer, and the fixed cost of keeping the tools honest.Support load, validation and re-verification effort per jurisdiction, and hosting cost per solve.
MilestonesStage gates with entry and exit criteria, not dates.Which gate is the real one: first paid pilot, first repeat customer, or first jurisdiction encoded end to end.
Funding pathWhat the next stage costs and which non-dilutive, customer or partner money could cover it.Eligibility, timing and cost share for each program — none of which is verified on this page.
RisksThe assumptions that would end the business if wrong, and the cheapest test of each.Whether the market wants the assurance product as much as the design product.

02

The arithmetic, as symbols

A plan eventually needs numbers. Until the evidence exists, the honest version names the inputs.

Revenue, unfilled

Annual revenue would be some combination of the number of subscribing firms, the price per firm per year, and the number of project or review engagements. Every one of those three quantities is currently unknown. We are deliberately not placing a plausible-looking value in any of them.

Cost, unfilled

The recurring cost per customer would depend on support hours per active user, the number of jurisdictions that must be kept current, the compute cost per simulation, and the share of engineering time that is maintenance rather than new capability. None of those has been measured yet.

What would make this real

One paid pilot with a documented before-and-after, one repeat customer who renews without prompting, and one jurisdiction whose rules are encoded, cited and independently checked end to end. Those three results would convert most of the blanks above into numbers worth reading.

Inputs to establish

  • Price anchor: what the alternative costs the customer today.
  • Customer count: from discovery, not from a market-size estimate.
  • Support load: measured during the pilot.
  • Cost to keep a jurisdiction current: measured on the next jurisdiction encoded.
  • Compute cost per solve: measurable now, on local hardware, before any cloud spend.
  • Churn or renewal: unknown until there is a second term to renew.

03

Stage gates, without dates

Each stage has an exit condition that can be judged by someone other than us.

StageExit condition
ValidationA third party reproduces a duct-loss result from a case we publish, including the caveat, and gets the same answer.
First paid pilotOne facility pays for a defined scope, and the deliverable is used in a real decision.
Repeatable deliveryA second customer of the same type receives the same scope without new engineering for each one.
Productised assuranceOne jurisdiction's rules are encoded, cited, independently reviewed, and a customer relies on the output in a review.
PartnershipA university or equipment partner co-funds a defined research question with a publishable or testable result.

These gates are ordered by dependency, not by schedule. No stage is estimated in weeks or dollars on this page.

04

What we would ask an advisor to pressure-test

The most useful outcome of a first advising conversation is a better set of questions.

Is the buyer the plant or the engineer?

The tool is used by an engineer but paid for by a facility, a firm or a corporate function. Which one, and does the person who feels the pain hold the budget?

Service first, or product first?

A services path reaches revenue sooner and teaches faster; a product path scales better but needs the assurance layer to be trusted. Which order is right for a two-person team?

Is assurance the wedge or the upsell?

Compliance evidence may be the sharper problem and the harder one to build. Is it the entry point or the expansion?

Repetition, on purpose. This page is aspirational. It is published to be corrected by an advisor, not to be believed as a plan.
