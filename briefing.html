Three working documents, and what they add up to

A public synthesis of an origin narrative, a value-proposition review and a pilot proposal. The point of putting them side by side is that they describe one business, not three: tools that compute, checks that cite, and a record that remembers.

02

The origin, condensed

Seven beats. The failures are kept because they are what produced the method.

The beats

  1. Make the engineering question runnable. The first artifact was an inspectable harness — decks, wrappers, logs, rendering, smoke tests — not a confident answer from a model.
  2. Discover that "it ran" is too weak. One flow solver exited cleanly with the velocity still at exactly zero because the boundary names selected no intended groups. Completion and the expected fields have been checked separately ever since.
  3. Make the setup understandable to the engineer. CAD-to-solve, load patches, face selection and setup previews grew out of the engineer's own markups. His markups, not a summary of them, are the acceptance test.
  4. Apply the evidence rule to requirements. Requirements became dated, cited, machine-checkable records. A missing measurement stays "not computable" instead of being filled with a plausible number.
  5. Expose the gaps between stages. Handoff contracts, review gates and a combined record appeared; the complete approval workflow did not. Duplicate checks were found and consolidated, which is how the gaps became visible at all.
  6. Put interactive math and geometry first. A 3D builder and mechanics guides give an early review surface before anyone pays for a full simulation — and the difficult experiment stays visible rather than being quietly dropped.
  7. Build toward a reviewed design record. The proposed next gate is: review inputs, geometry and calculations, run the appropriate prepared case, preserve the evidence, check the cited requirements, obtain human review.

What the origin actually proves

  • The reusable asset is the loop — prepare, run, inspect, correct — not any single model's answer.
  • Human judgment supplies the question, the geometry, the operating assumptions, the interpretation and the acceptance criteria.
  • Faster drafting moves effort into verification. That is an inference, not a measured productivity result.
  • Pictures and iteration counts do not prove a valid solution. Only a checked result does.

The origin document states plainly that it establishes no project-wide cost, savings or speedup figure, and that commit activity is an activity proxy rather than a measurement.

04

The third document: an engineering operations journal

A pilot proposal for the part of the problem that no solver touches — what the team knew, and when it knew it.

The problem

Knowledge leaves with the project

Project information is spread across mail, chat, transaction systems and folders. Open loops lose visibility, status is reconstructed in meetings, and the same question gets answered from memory.

The rule

The fifteen-second entry

Adoption fails if capture takes longer than the workaround. One common interaction — deliverable sent, approval received, manufacturing question, drawing correction, problem found, job released — is designed to take ten to fifteen seconds.

The payoff

One entry, five uses

A single entry updates project history, creates or closes an open loop, refreshes the dashboard, updates the search index, and creates the hook a future lesson-learned entry attaches to.

What the pilot proposes

A morning dashboard as the first screen of the day: open jobs, what is waiting on someone else, what is overdue, what is ready for release. A search that answers "have we seen this before?" from a job number in seconds rather than across four systems. A common lifecycle — open, active, waiting, blocked, closed, closeout — so engineering and the rest of the business share one vocabulary.

The scope discipline matters more than the feature list. The proposal puts email integration, transaction-system integration, ERP changes and automated mail parsing explicitly out of scope for phase one, and asks for a limited pilot on a small team with measured results before any expansion decision.

Why it belongs with the other two

The tools produce a result with its source attached. The journal produces the context around the result — who asked, what changed, what was decided and what is still open. Together they are the difference between a calculation and a defensible engineering record.

Stated limits

  • A proposal, not a deployment. No system is in production behind this page.
  • No savings measured. The proposal claims compounding value over time, not hours saved on day one.
  • Not a replacement for mail, chat or the transactional system of record.
  • No employer, system or customer is named anywhere in this synthesis.
05

What the three documents have in common

Read together, they describe one thesis rather than three projects.

Evidence discipline is the product

Every number is tied to a cited table, a solver log, a dated requirement or a validation case. The refusal to invent a missing value is the feature, not a limitation.

The record is what compounds

A duct layout, a stress plot, a requirement check and a project history all become assets only when someone else can re-open them and see why the answer was what it was.

Adoption beats completeness

Ten to fifteen seconds to capture an event, or a quick-look answer during a phone call. A tool that is slower than the spreadsheet does not get used, however capable it is.

What this page does not claim. No customers, revenue, market size, product certification or measured savings are claimed here. The three documents are the authors' own drafts; the origin, value and pilot material remains subject to correction by the people who did the work.

Last stop See it working

The business idea is described above; the engineering is on the showcase page — real screens, four flowcharts, and the limits next to every claim. The introduction is the short version if someone is starting from the bare domain.
